# 02 — Top-k pruning: is it exact, where did it fail, and how do engines page completely?

**Question.** Is block-max WAND / MaxScore exact top-k? What do the original papers say (Turtle and Flood 1995; Broder et al. 2003; Ding and Suel 2011) and later evaluations? Where did implementations return inexact results (Lucene, tantivy), including tantivy's multi-segment top-k? How do engines page deep results completely (search_after, scroll, point-in-time)?

**Credibility scale.** A = peer-reviewed paper, or primary engine source / javadoc / issue tracker. B = official docs, or an engineering write-up by the team that runs the system. C = secondary, used only to locate an A or B source.

**Short answer.** In the literature, MaxScore, WAND and block-max WAND are *rank-safe*: they return the same top k as exhaustive evaluation, provided every upper bound really is an upper bound and scores are combined the way the bounds assume. The guarantee covers the top k only, never "every match". In practice it has broken several times, each time through a bug, a bound assumption or float handling: Lucene's WANDScorer missed hits (fixed in 8.4); in tantivy 0.26.2 a DisjunctionMax under block-WAND is scored as a sum (fixed on main, not released); a tantivy union returned non-matching documents (fixed in 0.26.2). Complete deep paging needs a cursor over a total order plus a pinned snapshot: Elasticsearch has search_after with PIT, Lucene has searchAfter. tantivy has only `offset`.

## 1. What the papers guarantee

| # | Claim | Source | Cred. | Why this rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | MaxScore splits terms into *essential* and *non-essential* by their maximum impact. "No document containing only nonessential terms can make it into the top-k results." The method is "first proposed by Turtle and Flood". | Mallia, Siedlaczek, Suel, "An Experimental Study of Index Compression and DAAT Query Processing Methods", ECIR 2019, §3, https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~suel/papers/daat-ecir19.pdf | A | Peer-reviewed; PDF read in full |
| P2 | Turtle and Flood's own paper compares TAAT and DAAT and tests optimisations on full-text legal material. The paper is paywalled; I read only its abstract. | Turtle, Flood, "Query evaluation: strategies and optimizations", IP&M 31(6) 831–850, 1995, https://doi.org/10.1016/0306-4573(95)00020-H | A (abstract only) | Primary, but full text not reachable; its MaxScore safety is taken from P1 and P4 |
| P3 | WAND is a two-level process. The first level "identifies candidate documents using an approximate evaluation", and the second level fully scores them. "The amount of pruning can be controlled by the user". Full evaluations drop by more than 90% "almost without any loss in precision or recall". | Broder, Carmel, Herscovici, Soffer, Zien, "Efficient query evaluation using a two-level retrieval process", CIKM 2003, abstract at https://research.ibm.com/publications/efficient-query-evaluation-using-a-two-level-retrieval-process (DOI 10.1145/956863.956944) | A (abstract only) | Primary; the ACM PDF returned 403 |
| P4 | A survey defines four guarantee classes: *safe* (every document ranked and scored as unoptimised), *safe up to K / rank safe*, *set safe*, and *approximate*. "The WAND optimisation is safe up to K". Approximate WAND replaces θ by F·θ with F > 1 (Broder et al. 2003). MaxScore "is a safe up to K strategy". | Tonellotto, Macdonald, Ounis, "Efficient Query Processing for Scalable Web Search", FnTIR 12(4–5), 2018, §3, https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~craigm/publications/fnt-efficient-query-processing.pdf | A | Peer-reviewed survey by IR efficiency researchers; read in the authors' copy |
| P5 | Block-max WAND keeps a per-block maximum impact. It is safe: "exactly the same top-k results as the baseline in the same order and with the same score". WAND and the Strohman–Croft method are safe in the same sense. Skipping to a block boundary is "still guaranteeing a safe result". | Ding, Suel, "Faster top-k document retrieval using block-max indexes", SIGIR 2011, §2.6 and §4, https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~suel/papers/bmw.pdf | A | Original paper; PDF read in full |
| P6 | Measured on TREC GOV2 (25.2 M pages), top-10, TREC 2006 queries: exhaustive OR 225.7 ms, WAND 77.6 ms, Strohman–Croft 64.3 ms, BMW 27.9 ms. On TREC 2005 queries: 369.3, 64.4, 63.5 and 21.2 ms. | Ding and Suel 2011, Table 1 (as P5) | A | Same paper |
| P7 | "BlockMax WAND and … Variable BlockMax WAND … retrieve the top k … without any effectiveness degradation". Longer skipping makes short queries up to 37% faster (Gov2, ClueWeb09). | Mallia, Porciani, "Faster BlockMax WAND with Longer Skipping", ECIR 2019, https://www.antoniomallia.it/uploads/ECIR19a.pdf | A | Peer-reviewed; PDF read |
| P8 | Measured on Gov2 and ClueWeb09: "VBMW is the fastest query processing method for small k while MaxScore surpasses it for k > 100". "Query cost increases significantly with k". At k = 10 000 the algorithms converge. | Mallia et al., ECIR 2019 (as P1), §4 and §5 | A | Peer-reviewed, reproducible (PISA) |
| P9 | Lucene moved top-level disjunctions from block-max WAND to block-max MAXSCORE in July 2022. WAND "typically evaluates fewer hits than MAXSCORE but with a higher per-hit overhead". | Adrien Grand (Elastic / Lucene PMC), "More skipping with block-max MAXSCORE", Elastic Search Labs, 2023-12-06, https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/more-skipping-with-bm-maxscore | B | Written by the implementer |

**Two conditions follow from these sources:**
- The guarantee holds only for a monotone score that is a sum of per-term contributions, each bounded by a stored maximum.
- It covers the top k, not every match.

Lucene states the monotonicity condition as a contract. "Score must not decrease when `freq` increases", and must not increase when `norm` increases. The maximum is "bound by `score(Float.MAX_VALUE, 1)`" (Lucene 10.3 `Similarity.SimScorer` javadoc, https://lucene.apache.org/core/10_3_0/core/org/apache/lucene/search/similarities/Similarity.SimScorer.html; A).

## 2. Where implementations were not exact

| # | Case | Source | Cred. | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Lucene WANDScorer missed hits.** Grand: "There is a rare bug in WANDScorer … it might skip over hits thinking that they can't have a competitive score when in fact they could." It was found while building the 8.4 release candidate and fixed in 8.4. | LUCENE-9103 / apache/lucene#10145, 2019, https://github.com/apache/lucene/issues/10145 | A | Engine issue tracker, by the maintainer |
| I2 | **Lucene's float handling inside WAND.** WANDScorer "operates on longs to avoid accuracy issues with floating-point numbers". It scales scores conservatively, so the inaccuracy costs skipping, not hits. | LUCENE-10503 (#11539), https://github.com/apache/lucene/issues/11539; LUCENE-10121 (#11158), https://github.com/apache/lucene/issues/11158 | A | Issue tracker |
| I3 | **Lucene's handling of 0 and +∞ in the WAND scaling was "backwards".** It caused a failed assertion when a clause reported `MAX_VALUE` as its maximum score. Fixed in 9.2. | LUCENE-10553 (#11589), https://github.com/apache/lucene/issues/11589 | A | Issue tracker |
| I4 | **Lucene's pruning is tied to its doc-id tie-break.** The collector raises the minimum competitive score to `Math.nextUp(score)` "since we tie-break on doc id and collect in doc id order". Results are "sorted by score descending and then (when the scores are tied) docID ascending". Ties are therefore exact only under Lucene's own (score, docID) order. | `TopScoreDocCollector.java` lines 26 and 137–164, Lucene 10.5.2, https://github.com/apache/lucene/blob/releases/lucene/10.5.2/lucene/core/src/java/org/apache/lucene/search/TopScoreDocCollector.java | A | Source |
| I5 | **Lucene's hit count is a lower bound by default.** `IndexSearcher` counts "hits accurately up to 1000". Above the `totalHitsThreshold`, `totalHits` is "a lower bound of the hit count". `Integer.MAX_VALUE` makes the count exact but "likely make[s] query processing slower". | `IndexSearcher.java:95-99` (10.5.2); `TopScoreDocCollectorManager` javadoc, https://lucene.apache.org/core/10_3_0/core/org/apache/lucene/search/TopScoreDocCollectorManager.html | A | Source and javadoc |
| I6 | **Elasticsearch's hit count is a lower bound above 10 000.** The default is `DEFAULT_TRACK_TOTAL_HITS_UP_TO = 10000`, and `TRACK_TOTAL_HITS_ACCURATE = Integer.MAX_VALUE`. Responses carry `relation: "gte"` for a lower bound and `"eq"` for an exact count. | `SearchContext.java:72-74`, elastic/elasticsearch main, 2026-10 (latest release 9.5.5), https://github.com/elastic/elasticsearch/blob/main/server/src/main/java/org/elasticsearch/search/internal/SearchContext.java; track_total_hits reference, https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/7.0/search-request-track-total-hits.html | A / B | Source, plus the vendor's docs |
| I7 | **tantivy 0.26.2: DisjunctionMax under block-WAND is scored as a sum.** "`BooleanWeight::for_each_pruning` hands a union of term scorers straight to `block_wand`, whatever the weight's score combiner." The result: "A `DisjunctionMaxQuery` collected through `TopDocs` therefore scores a document matching in two fields as the **sum** … and ignores the tie breaker". It hides because `Basic` postings take a different path. The fix was merged to main on 2026-08-07 and is **not in 0.26.2**: 0.26.2 was published 2026-09-08, its changelog lists three other fixes, and `SUPPORTS_BLOCK_WAND` is absent from its source. An independent probe on 0.26.2 measured 4.44 under `TopDocs` against 2.22 correct. | quickwit-oss/tantivy PR #3022, https://github.com/quickwit-oss/tantivy/pull/3022; CHANGELOG.md on main, https://github.com/quickwit-oss/tantivy/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md; crates.io version list; `tantivy-0.26.2/src/query/boolean_query/boolean_weight.rs:50-75` | A | Merged PR with a regression test; crate source |
| I8 | **tantivy 0.26.1: a union returned documents that do not match.** `BufferedUnionScorer::seek` keeps a child that `seek_danger` left invalid, "so a document holding one term of an AND child is returned by the union". It was hit on a 16.35 M document data set with a DisMax of per-field ANDs under a filter. Fixed in 0.26.2 ("Disable the buffered union `seek_danger` override", #3086). | tantivy issue #3086, 2026-09-08, https://github.com/quickwit-oss/tantivy/issues/3086; CHANGELOG 0.26.2 | A | Issue tracker with a reproduction |
| I9 | **tantivy's own block-WAND equivalence test is approximate and partly disabled.** It asserts scores are `nearly_equals` (relative 1e-4), not equal. The three-term property test is `#[ignore]`d. A known "BM25 failing case in CI" (#1981, 2023) is still open. So pruned and unpruned f32 scores are not asserted bit-identical, and summation order can differ. | `tantivy-0.26.2/src/query/boolean_query/block_wand.rs:335-337, 435-495, 621-624`; issue #1981, https://github.com/quickwit-oss/tantivy/issues/1981 | A | Source and tracker |
| I10 | **tantivy's top-N with ties used to depend on segment order.** The old `TopNComputer` threshold did not include the `DocId`, "a determinism hazard … some might be eliminated non-deterministically based on the order that segments were collected". The current code admits equal items to the buffer and is deterministic, at a cost: ParadeDB measured a 60% slowdown on many equal values. | tantivy issue #2775, 2025-12, https://github.com/quickwit-oss/tantivy/issues/2775; PR #2672 review, https://github.com/quickwit-oss/tantivy/pull/2672 | A | Tracker discussion by maintainers and a downstream engine team |
| I11 | **tantivy across segments is exact, with no shared threshold.** `TopDocs` collects `doc_range.end` = offset + limit per segment. Each segment starts pruning at `Score::MIN`, and `merge_fruits` merges the per-segment top lists. Lucene instead shares a global minimum competitive score across slices (`MaxScoreAccumulator`). Both are exact for top k. tantivy prunes less per segment. | `tantivy-0.26.2/src/collector/sort_key/sort_by_score.rs:39-52`, `sort_key_top_collector.rs:54-69`; Lucene `MaxScoreAccumulator.java` (10.5.2) | A | Source |
| I12 | **tantivy pruning reaches only plain term unions.** Constant-score automaton queries (regex, fuzzy, range) have "no early termination: `TopDocs::with_limit(N)` is `O(matches)`" (#2974, open). Block-WAND for `BoostWeight` is still open (#1034). | tantivy issues #2974, #1034, #2390 | A | Tracker |

## 3. Paging deep results completely

| # | Engine | Mechanism | Source | Cred. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Lucene | `IndexSearcher.searchAfter(after, query, n)`: "By passing the bottom result from a previous page as `after`, this method can be used for efficient 'deep-paging'". The cursor is a (score, docID) pair, so it is valid only against the same reader. | `IndexSearcher.java:575-599` (10.5.2) | A |
| D2 | Elasticsearch | from + size is capped at 10 000 by default (`index.max_result_window`). "We no longer recommend using the scroll API for deep pagination". Use `search_after` with a point in time (PIT). "All PIT search requests add an implicit sort tiebreaker field called `_shard_doc`", unique and constant within the PIT. Without a PIT, "the order of your results may change, causing inconsistent results across pages", and pages "could miss or duplicate hits". | Elastic, "Paginate search results", current docs, https://www.elastic.co/docs/reference/elasticsearch/rest-apis/paginate-search-results | B |
| D3 | tantivy | Only `TopDocs::and_offset(offset)`: "equivalent to `OFFSET` in MySQL or PostgreSQL and `start` in Lucene's TopDocsCollector". There is no `search_after` anywhere in the crate. Each page re-collects offset + limit per segment, so page p costs O(p·size) memory per segment and its work grows with depth. A held `Searcher` is the only snapshot. Ties fall to ascending `DocAddress`. | docs.rs TopDocs 0.26.2, https://docs.rs/tantivy/0.26.2/tantivy/collector/struct.TopDocs.html; a ripgrep for search_after over `tantivy-0.26.2/src` returns nothing | A / B |
| D4 | tantivy (next) | 0.27.0 (unreleased, on main) adds `SchemaBuilder::add_tie_breaker_field`. Values are generated "consecutive within a segment, start at a random offset, and are preserved across merges", and are now i64 "because downstream cursor protocols represent tie-breakers as signed integers". Ties become stable across merges, but are **not derived from content**: a fresh build draws new random offsets. | PRs #3104 / #3143 (merged 2026-10-02), #3158 (2026-10-07); CHANGELOG 0.27.0 on main | A |

**Evidence is thin where:**
- No paper measures how often real implementations violate rank safety. Every case above is a single bug report.
- Turtle and Flood 1995 and Broder et al. 2003 were read as abstracts only; their safety statements come through the 2011 and 2018 papers.
- No source measures pruning on code-search corpora; every number above is from web collections (GOV2, ClueWeb09).
- No benchmark covers "complete" (k = all matches) retrieval. P8 shows only that the pruning advantage shrinks as k grows, up to k = 10 000.

## What this means for codectx

- **Rule 3 no top-k cap — supports.** Every pruning method is safe only *up to K* (P4, P5). At k = every match there is nothing to prune, and P8 shows the advantage already shrinking at large k. Both engines' default hit counts are lower bounds (I5, I6). A complete answer cannot use `TopDocs` pruning. The product's choice of an unpruned `collect_segment` (`for_each`, never `for_each_pruning`) is the only path that is exact *and* complete. It also stays clear of every pruning defect found here (I1, I7, I9).
- **Rule 7 f64 BM25F, one saturation — supports, with a caveat.** BM25F's single saturation over the weighted tf is not a sum of per-field scores. So the per-term max-score bound that WAND needs would have to be computed on the combined weighted tf. tantivy's block maxima are per field, and I7 shows what happens when a combiner that is not a sum meets block-WAND: wrong scores. Scoring outside tantivy's pruning path, as the product mode does, is the safe choice.
- **Rule 4 content-only deterministic ties — supports.** Lucene's pruning is exact only under its (score, docID) order (I4). tantivy ties on `DocAddress` (D3), and its upcoming tie-breaker field is random per segment (D4). A pruned top k in either engine therefore depends on doc ids. In tantivy's own test the pruned and unpruned f32 scores differ within 1e-4 (I9), which can reorder near-ties. None of this is content-derived, so the rule cannot be met by stock pruning.
- **Rule 11 1e-6 quantization to int64 — supports.** Lucene had to move WAND to scaled longs to avoid float trouble (I2, I3). tantivy compares f32 within a 1e-4 tolerance (I9). Quantizing an f64 score before any comparison removes the summation-order sensitivity that both engines show.
- **Rule 10 keyset-cursor paging — supports.** The production answer to deep paging is a cursor over a total order on a pinned snapshot: ES search_after + PIT + `_shard_doc`, and Lucene `searchAfter` (D1, D2). ES documents the missed or duplicated hits you get without a PIT. tantivy offers only offset (D3), so a tantivy-backed tier still needs codectx's own keyset cursor over the content-derived order.
- **Rule 5 retained snapshots — supports.** ES's PIT exists precisely because consistent pages need a pinned view (D2). It is the same requirement as rule 5.
- **Rule 9 tier first, then lexical score — neutral.** No source here bears on it. Note only that a tier-first order is a sort key ahead of score, so score pruning would apply only within a tier.
- **Rule 1, rule 2, rule 6, rule 8 — neutral** for this question (see 03 for rule 1, rule 2 and rule 6).
- **Adopting tantivy 0.26.2:** do not use `DisjunctionMaxQuery` with `TopDocs` on freq-reading fields (I7) until a release carries PR #3022. Pin to 0.26.2 or later for the union fix (I8).
